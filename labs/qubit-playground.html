Qubit Playground

A real (if tiny) quantum simulator. Your qubit starts as |0⟩. Apply gates, watch the two amplitudes move, including going negative, and then measure it.

Intermediate~15 minNot completed yet

Your circuit

Tip: H on |0⟩ gives an equal superposition. What happens if you apply H twice?

State

The arrow's x-position is the amplitude of |0⟩ and its y-position is the amplitude of |1⟩. It always sits on the circle, because the probabilities must add up to 1.

Amplitudes & probabilities

Amplitudes can be negative. Probabilities are amplitudes squared, so the sign is invisible until two paths meet and interfere.

Run it 100 times

Measuring destroys the superposition, so real quantum computers run a circuit many times and count the results.

Why this matters

  • H · H = do nothing. After the first H the qubit is "both". After the second, the two routes to |1⟩ have opposite signs and cancel. That's interference, the engine behind every quantum speed-up.
  • Signs you can't see still matter. |+⟩ and |−⟩ give identical 50/50 measurements, yet one H turns them into different definite answers.
  • Real algorithms scale this up. Shor's and Grover's algorithms use many qubits to arrange interference so wrong answers cancel and right answers reinforce.
