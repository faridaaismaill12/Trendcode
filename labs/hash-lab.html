2. Mine a block (proof of work)

Because a hash can't be predicted, the only way to find one that starts with zeros is to keep trying. Miners add a counter called a nonce to their data and hash again, and again, and again. Each extra zero makes it about 16× harder.

What you just saw

  • Unpredictability is the point. If you could guess how the hash changes, you could forge data that matches a fingerprint. The avalanche effect makes that hopeless.
  • Hard to find, easy to check. Mining took thousands of tries, but anyone can verify your answer with a single hash. That asymmetry is what proof-of-work and password hashing rely on.
  • Real mining is the same idea at a vast scale. Bitcoin's network computes hundreds of quintillions of hashes per second, with a difficulty far beyond the 5 zeros here.
