Zero-knowledge proofs

Proving that something is true, such as “I'm over 18” or “this transaction is valid”, without revealing anything else.

🔐 Cryptography Intermediate 3 min read

01In plain words

Suppose you want to prove to a friend that you know the password to a locked door, without telling them the password. You could walk in through one door and come out of another every time they ask. After enough rounds they'd be convinced, yet they'd have learned nothing about the password itself. A zero-knowledge proof (ZKP) is a mathematical version of this: it convinces a verifier that a statement is true while revealing only that it's true.

02Why it matters

Much of life online forces us to over-share: showing a whole ID card to prove your age, or exposing a full balance to prove you can pay. ZKPs make “prove, don't reveal” possible. They also let blockchains and cloud services verify that a heavy computation was done correctly without redoing it.

03How it works

  1. Three guarantees. A ZKP must be complete (an honest prover always convinces), sound (a cheater almost never does) and zero-knowledge (the verifier learns nothing beyond the statement's truth).
  2. Turn the claim into maths. The statement (“I know a secret x such that hash(x) = y”) is encoded as a set of equations, often called a circuit.
  3. Commit and challenge. In interactive proofs, the prover commits to values, the verifier sends random challenges, and the prover answers. A cheater gets caught with high probability.
  4. Make it non-interactive. The Fiat–Shamir trick replaces the verifier's random challenges with a hash, producing a single proof anyone can check later.
  5. Pick a proof system. zk-SNARKs give tiny proofs that verify in milliseconds (some need a one-time “trusted setup”). zk-STARKs need no trusted setup and rely only on hashes, at the cost of larger proofs.

04Real-world example

Private payments and rollups

Zcash (launched 2016) uses zk-SNARKs so a transaction can be verified as valid, with no money created from nothing, while hiding sender, receiver and amount. On Ethereum, “zk-rollups” bundle thousands of transactions off-chain and post one small proof that they were all processed correctly. Digital identity projects are piloting ZKPs to prove facts like “over 18” from a credential without revealing the birth date.

05Advantages & limitations

Advantages

  • Data minimisation: reveal a fact, not the data behind it.
  • Verification is fast and cheap even for huge computations.
  • Proofs are portable: anyone can verify, with no need to trust the prover.
  • STARK-style proofs built on hashes are believed to be quantum-resistant.

Limitations

  • Generating proofs is computationally heavy, often thousands of times slower than just running the computation.
  • Writing circuits is specialised and error-prone. A bug can make a “proof” accept false statements.
  • Some SNARKs need a trusted setup ceremony. If its secret randomness leaked, fake proofs could be forged.

06Security & privacy implications

  • Soundness bugs are catastrophic and silent, so audits and formal verification of circuits are essential.
  • Zero-knowledge hides the witness, not the metadata: who submits a proof, when and how often can still leak information.
  • Trusted-setup ceremonies are usually run with many participants so that only one needs to be honest.
