Model inversion attacks

Recovering private information about a model's training data just by querying the model.

🤖 AI Advanced 3 min read

01In plain words

A trained model is a compressed summary of its training data, and sometimes that summary gives away too much. In a model inversion attack, someone repeatedly queries a model and uses its answers to reconstruct what the training data looked like: a recognisable face, a sensitive medical attribute, or even word-for-word text the model memorised.

02Why it matters

Organisations often assume that sharing a model (or an API to it) is safe because the raw data stays private. Inversion and related attacks show that models themselves can be personal data. This matters for privacy law, for medical and facial-recognition systems, and for large language models trained on scraped text.

03How it works

  1. Query and observe: the attacker sends inputs and records outputs, especially confidence scores.
  2. Optimise: starting from noise, they adjust an input step by step to maximise the model's confidence for a target class, such as a person's name, producing an image that resembles the training examples.
  3. Infer attributes: combining partial knowledge about a person with model outputs can reveal a hidden sensitive attribute.
  4. Related attacks: membership inference tells whether a specific record was in the training set, and training-data extraction gets generative models to output memorised text.

04Real-world example

Faces and memorised text

In 2015, Fredrikson and colleagues reconstructed recognisable face images from a facial-recognition model using only a person's name and the model's confidence scores. Earlier work inferred patients' genetic markers from a drug-dosing model. In 2021, researchers extracted memorised training text, including names, phone numbers and email addresses, from the GPT-2 language model.

05Advantages & limitations

Advantages

  • Studying these attacks has driven better privacy-preserving training methods.
  • Gives organisations concrete tests to audit models for leakage before release.

Limitations

  • Hard to rule out completely: any useful model reveals something about its data.
  • Defences such as strong differential privacy can reduce model accuracy.

06Security & privacy implications

  • Return labels instead of detailed confidence scores where possible, and rate-limit queries.
  • Train with differential privacy for formal guarantees, and deduplicate and scrub training data, since repeated records are memorised more.
  • Treat models trained on personal data as personal data under privacy law, with the same access controls.
