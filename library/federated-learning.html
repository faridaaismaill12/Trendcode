Federated learning

Training one AI model across many devices or organisations without collecting their raw data in one place.

🤖 AI Intermediate 3 min read

01In plain words

Normally, to train a model you gather all the data on one server. Federated learning flips this: the model travels to the data instead. Each phone or hospital trains a copy on its own data, and only the lessons learned (model updates) are sent back and combined. The raw data never leaves home.

02Why it matters

Some of the most valuable data is also the most private: what you type on your phone, patient scans, bank transactions. Laws, contracts and common sense often forbid pooling it. Federated learning lets organisations and devices benefit from shared learning while keeping data local.

03How it works

  1. Broadcast: a central server sends the current model to a sample of participating clients.
  2. Train locally: each client improves the model on its own data for a few steps.
  3. Send updates: clients send back only the changes to the model's weights.
  4. Aggregate: the server averages the updates, weighted by how much data each client had (the “Federated Averaging” algorithm, McMahan et al., 2017), to produce a better global model.
  5. Protect updates: secure aggregation lets the server see only the combined sum, not any individual update, and differential privacy can add noise for formal guarantees.

04Real-world example

Your phone keyboard

Google's Gboard keyboard was one of the first large-scale uses (announced in 2017): next-word suggestions improve by learning from typing on millions of phones, without the text being uploaded. In healthcare, the Federated Tumor Segmentation (FeTS) initiative trained brain-tumour models across dozens of hospitals worldwide without sharing patient scans.

05Advantages & limitations

Advantages

  • Raw data stays on the device or inside the organisation.
  • Unlocks learning from data that could never be centralised.
  • Less data transfer than uploading full datasets.

Limitations

  • Client data is uneven (not “IID”), which makes training slower and less stable.
  • Communication and coordination across many unreliable devices is complex.
  • Harder to debug: you can't inspect the training data.

06Security & privacy implications

  • Updates can leak data: “gradient inversion” attacks (for example Deep Leakage from Gradients, 2019) can reconstruct training examples from individual updates, which is why secure aggregation and differential privacy matter.
  • Malicious clients can poison the global model or plant backdoors, so robust aggregation and anomaly detection help.
  • Federated isn't automatically private. It's a strong foundation that needs extra privacy-enhancing technologies on top.
