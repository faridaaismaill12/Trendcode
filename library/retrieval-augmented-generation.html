Retrieval-Augmented Generation

Letting an AI look things up in your documents before it answers, so replies are current and cite sources.

🤖 AI Intermediate 3 min read

01In plain words

A language model only “knows” what was in its training data, which may be out of date and never included your company's documents. Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) gives it an open-book exam: before answering, the system searches a collection of documents for relevant passages and pastes them into the prompt. The model then answers using that material and can point to where each fact came from.

02Why it matters

RAG is the most common way businesses make AI useful on their own knowledge: help-desk articles, policies, product manuals, research papers. It reduces made-up answers (hallucinations), keeps information current without retraining, and makes answers checkable through citations.

03How it works

  1. Ingest: documents are split into chunks of a few paragraphs.
  2. Embed: each chunk is turned into a vector, a list of numbers that captures its meaning, using an embedding model.
  3. Index: vectors are stored in a vector database (often combined with keyword search).
  4. Retrieve: the user's question is embedded the same way, and the closest chunks are fetched, optionally re-ranked for relevance.
  5. Augment and generate: the retrieved chunks are added to the prompt with instructions to answer from them and cite sources.

04Real-world example

An internal help-desk assistant

An employee asks, “How many days of parental leave do I get?” The RAG system retrieves the two relevant paragraphs from the HR policy and the country-specific appendix, and the model answers with the number of days and links to both documents. When HR updates the policy, re-indexing the document updates the answers immediately, with no model retraining.

05Advantages & limitations

Advantages

  • Up-to-date answers without retraining the model.
  • Citations make answers verifiable.
  • Much cheaper and faster to set up than fine-tuning.
  • Access to knowledge can follow existing document permissions.

Limitations

  • Answers are only as good as retrieval. Bad chunking or search returns the wrong context.
  • Models can still misread or ignore the retrieved text.
  • Adds latency and infrastructure: an embedding model, a vector store and a re-indexing pipeline.

06Security & privacy implications

  • Indirect prompt injection: a poisoned document in the index can carry hidden instructions to the model.
  • Permission leaks: enforce access control at retrieval time, or users may get answers drawn from documents they shouldn't see.
  • Embeddings aren't anonymous. Research in 2023 showed that text can often be largely reconstructed from its embedding vectors, so protect the vector store like the documents themselves.
