Agentic AI

AI that doesn't just answer questions, but plans and takes actions toward a goal.

🤖 AI Beginner 3 min read

01In plain words

A chatbot waits for your question and replies. Agentic AI is handed a goal, like “book me a train to Leeds under £50” or “fix this failing test”, and works out the steps itself: searching, clicking, running code, checking the result and trying again. The “agentic” part is about autonomy: how much the system decides and does on its own.

02Why it matters

Agentic systems can automate multi-step work that used to need a person at every step, such as research, coding, data entry and customer support. That's a major productivity shift. But an AI that can act can also cause real damage when it's wrong or manipulated, so safety, permissions and oversight become central design questions.

03How it works

  1. A goal. The user (or another system) gives an objective, plus constraints such as budget, deadlines or rules.
  2. A reasoning core. Usually a large language model breaks the goal into steps and decides what to do next.
  3. Tools. The model can call functions: web search, a browser, a code runner, email, databases, company APIs. Standards like the Model Context Protocol (MCP) make connecting tools easier.
  4. A loop. Act, observe the result, update the plan, repeat, until the goal is met or the agent decides to stop and ask for help.
  5. Guardrails. Permissions, spending limits, human approval for risky steps, logging and the ability to stop the agent.

04Real-world example

Coding agents

Software teams now use coding agents that read a repository, edit several files, run the test suite, read the failures and iterate until the tests pass, then open a pull request for a human to review. The same pattern appears in IT support agents that reset accounts and in research agents that read dozens of sources and write a summary.

05Advantages & limitations

Advantages

  • Automates tedious, multi-step tasks end to end.
  • Can work continuously and in parallel.
  • Adapts to unexpected situations better than rigid scripts.

Limitations

  • Errors compound: a wrong early step can derail everything after it.
  • Harder to predict and test than single responses.
  • Costly in compute, and slower than a direct answer.
  • Unclear accountability when an autonomous system makes a mistake.

06Security & privacy implications

  • Prompt injection is the top risk: instructions hidden in web pages, emails or files can hijack an agent that reads them.
  • Excessive agency, giving an agent more permissions than the task needs, is one of OWASP's top risks for LLM applications. Apply least privilege and require human approval for irreversible actions.
  • Agents often hold credentials and access private data, so log every action and keep secrets scoped and short-lived.
